Yeah — I think this is probably the next major design problem for Synthetic Ascension.

Right now you've built a pretty deep item economy: rarity, rolls, POS/NEG, sets, augments, Luck, merging. But there's still a risk that the actual combat outcome is:

Build A: I walk around shooting and have +340% Power.
Build B: I walk around shooting and have +210% Power, +80% Haste and lifesteal.
Build C: I walk around shooting and happen to be cursed.

Those are mathematically different builds, but they aren't different playstyles.

The missing layer is behavioral items.

The rule I'd use

An important item should change one of these things:

what you do → when you do it → where you do it → what you target → what you're willing to sacrifice.

And the strongest build-defining items should sometimes close doors, not just give benefits.

That's crucial.

If every item says:

+X, and also here's another bonus,

then the optimal playstyle remains "do what I was already doing, harder."

But an item saying:

You can no longer do X normally. Instead, X works like this...

can create an actual build.

I'd split the item ecosystem conceptually like this
Type	Job
Stat items	Make the current build stronger
Catalysts	Introduce a new mechanic
Mutators	Change how an existing mechanic behaves
Converters	Turn one resource/mechanic into another
Payoffs	Reward a particular behavior
Keystones	Fundamentally alter the rules, usually with a drawback

You don't need to show these categories to the player.

They're a design tool.

Maybe only 20–30% of the item pool needs to be genuinely behavioral. The rest can support those items.

The big thing: make builds care about POSITIONING

This is one of the easiest ways to make two builds actually feel different.

Imagine the same ranged weapon.

Normal build:

kite enemies and shoot.

One item could create:

Doctrine of Stillness

After standing still for 1.5 seconds, attacks gain massive penetration and range.

Moving removes the effect.

Suddenly you're playing:

find safe firing lane → plant yourself → unload → panic and relocate.

Another could do the opposite:

Pilgrim's Momentum

Every metre travelled without stopping adds Momentum.

Momentum increases attack speed and periodically causes your attack to Echo.

Stop moving for one second and it decays rapidly.

Now you're playing:

constantly weave through the map and maintain motion.

Same weapon.

Completely different game.

And those two items naturally hate each other.

That's good.

Movement itself should become buildcraft

Right now movement is probably mostly:

avoid damage.

Items can make movement part of offense.

For example:

Vector Halo

Your ranged projectiles no longer immediately fire forward.

They accumulate in an orbit around Syn'Tek.

Dashing launches all stored projectiles in the dash direction.

Now your attack loop becomes:

attack → build halo → position → dash → shotgun apocalypse.

An upgrade item might then say:

Halo projectiles orbit faster and damage enemies they pass through.

Another might say:

Launch only half the Halo on dash; remaining projectiles reverse orbit.

You've gone from:

ranged attack +50%

to an actual mechanical build.

Target selection can become part of builds too

Most horde games eventually reduce target choice to:

murder whatever is closest.

You have elites, objectives, huge populations and events. Use them.

For example:

Predestination Sigil

Your first hit against an unmarked elite marks it.

While a Mark exists:

deal much more damage to the marked enemy;
deal slightly less damage to everything else;
killing the marked enemy releases a large effect.

Now the player actively looks through the horde for:

THAT FUCKER.

Another item could do exactly the opposite:

damage falls dramatically against enemies you've already hit, but first hits explode.

That's now an AoE crowd-tagging build where you want to spread attacks around rather than focus fire.

Same Power stat.

Different decisions.

Attack rhythm is another huge opportunity

You can change when the player wants to attack.

For instance:

Third Litany

Every third attack is massively empowered.

But attacking again before the third attack's effect resolves cancels its bonus.

Now Haste isn't automatically good.

The player starts playing around a rhythm.

Or:

Stored Violence

While you are not attacking, Power accumulates.

Your next attack releases all stored Power.

Suddenly the build wants:

dodge → dodge → dodge → enormous hit.

That could make a melee character play almost like a counterattacker.

Resources can fundamentally change playstyle

You've already accidentally built an excellent foundation for this because Synthetic Ascension has unusual resources:

HP, Followers, Luck, Burden, Resonance.

Items should abuse them.

Followers in particular are amazing because they already mean:

money + lives + belief/power.

Imagine an item:

Tithe Furnace

Every X attacks, you may consume a Follower to massively empower the attack.

If you have fewer than your reconstruction cost in Followers, the Furnace refuses to consume them.

Now suddenly:

Do I spend my literal believers as ammunition?

And another item might say:

Tithe Furnace can consume Followers below reconstruction threshold, but each one dramatically increases its output.

Now you've got an actual suicidal cult build.

That's not +20 Power.

That's a different relationship with the whole run economy.

HP can become something other than "don't reach zero"

Litany of Wounds is already starting down this road.

You can take it much further.

Imagine one build where:

healing is safety.

Another where:

healing is damage.

Another where:

healing is undesirable.

For example:

Martyr Circuit

Above 70% HP:

reduced attack speed.

Below 70%:

normal.

Below 40%:

major Haste.

Below 20%:

attacks begin producing synthetic echoes.

Now suddenly health pickups create an actual decision:

Fuck, I don't WANT that heal right now.

That's playstyle.

And melee lifesteal becomes something you actively manage rather than simply "more sustain = better."

Conversion items are probably your strongest future category

These are where really weird builds happen.

You've already done this conceptually with Inversion Lens:

curse → positive value.

Do the same elsewhere.

Examples:

Crit Chance → projectile count, but you can no longer normally crit.

Armor → melee Power, but Armor contributes less defensively.

Movement Speed → attack range, creating a slow artillery build.

Excess healing → barrier, allowing regeneration builds.

Evasion → counterattack chance, meaning dodging becomes offense.

Luck → volatility, where high Luck increases both jackpot effects and dangerous outcomes rather than simply improving averages.

Followers → summon strength, creating something approaching a cult/minion archetype.

A converter immediately makes the player look at previously irrelevant items differently.

That's exactly what you just achieved with NEG.

Sets should eventually change mechanics, not just give bonuses

Gravemarch mutating into a life-drain aura is already a much better direction than:

4 pieces: +15 Armor
6 pieces: +25 Power.

I'd make the strongest set tiers often transform something.

Maybe a ranged set eventually makes projectiles:

remain embedded in enemies and detonate when you dash.

A melee set could make:

overkill damage continue through the enemy in a line.

A magic set could make:

every spell leaves a sigil at the cast point, and the third sigil connects the previous two.

Now completing a set isn't:

yay, more DPS.

It's:

oh shit, the build just came online.

You also have an underused axis: PLAYING THE MAP

This is something Synthetic Ascension can do that Vampire Survivors-style games often can't.

Your items can interact with:

buildings;
secondaries;
Wardstones;
Resonance;
Threat;
Exit Rite;
exploration.

For example:

Heretical Cartography

Entering an unexplored building temporarily increases Luck and Power.

Clearing it extends the effect.

Now the build wants to explore aggressively.

Another:

Siege Doctrine

While within a secondary-objective area:

significant defensive bonus + special attacks.

Outside it:

nothing.

Now secondaries aren't merely rewards.

Your build fucking loves them.

Another:

Overtime Gospel

Once Resonance reaches 100%, you gain escalating Power every 30 seconds you refuse to leave.

Threat also increases faster.

That's a beautiful greed build:

Exit Rite is open.
I should leave.
...but look at my damage.

That fits your game's core loop ridiculously well.

Luck should create some truly insane behavioral builds

Currently Luck bending probabilities is good.

But eventually I'd want a few items that say:

I am a Luck build.

Not merely:

I have 73 Luck.

For example:

Loaded Providence

Normal crit chance is disabled.

Every attack instead rolls a Lucky Crit.

Lucky Crit chance is lower, but Lucky Crits trigger every Luck-based proc you have simultaneously.

Now suddenly:

Lucky Charm;
Gambler's Rite;
lucky drops;
lucky evasion;
other Luck items

start forming a machine.

And a different Luck item could say:

whenever you fail a Luck roll, gain "Misfortune"; your next successful Luck proc scales with stored Misfortune.

Now bad luck itself becomes buildup.

That's much more thematic than simply raising probability from 17% to 24%.

I think builds should sometimes have "engines"

This might be the most important thing I'd add.

Not every synergy should simply be:

Item A gives +Power because Item B exists.

Instead:

A creates something → B uses it → C modifies the result.

For example:

Item A: Crits create synthetic shards.
Item B: Picking up shards grants temporary Haste.
Item C: At maximum Haste, further shards instead orbit you and damage enemies.
Set effect: Orbiting shards are launched when you evade an attack.

That's a build engine.

The player can understand:

crit → shards → haste → orbit → evade → launch.

And then when the screen explodes they know why.

That's what your Run Sheet eventually needs to explain.

One very important constraint

Don't make every item its own unique mini-game.

You'll end up with 80 unrelated mechanics and an impossible codebase.

I would establish a relatively small set of universal gameplay events/hooks underneath everything:

Hook	What can build around it
Attack	rhythm, charge, alternate attacks
Hit	marks, chains, status, resource generation
Kill	explosions, Followers, momentum
Crit / Lucky Crit	proc engines
Damage taken	retaliation, low-HP mechanics
Evade	counters
Distance moved	momentum
Dash	stored attacks / releases
Healing	barrier/conversion
Follower gained/spent	belief builds
Resonance gained	ascension builds
Secondary completed	objective builds
NEG burden changed	curse builds
Item acquired/merged	Gambler/crafting builds

Then items listen to and modify these shared events.

That gives you hundreds of possible combinations without writing hundreds of isolated systems.

And I think the strongest items need drawbacks

This is probably the biggest philosophical change I'd make.

A playstyle only becomes truly different if the player sacrifices something.

Compare:

+40% damage while moving.

versus:

You deal 50% less damage while stationary. While moving, attacks accumulate Momentum and eventually become much stronger.

The second one actually tells you how to play.

Or:

+30% damage to elites.

versus:

Your damage to ordinary enemies is reduced, but marked elites take enormous damage and explode into a chain reaction when killed.

Now you're an elite hunter.

So I'd actively design some items around:

"You are no longer good at X; in exchange, you become absurd at Y."

POS/NEG already trained the game for exactly this kind of thinking.

What I'd want Synthetic Ascension's finished builds to feel like

Two players using the same starting ranged weapon should plausibly end up with:

Player 1: constantly sprinting, storing projectiles in a halo and releasing them through dashes.

Player 2: barely moving, finding firing lanes and deleting elites with giant charged shots.

Player 3: deliberately sitting at 25% HP, using Burden and lifesteal to ride the edge of death.

Player 4: hoarding Followers until an event, then burning belief itself to fuel enormous attacks.

Player 5: running into every optional building because exploration generates Luck, Followers and escalating bonuses.

Player 6: carrying two absolutely horrific NEG items because Corruption Engine turns them into the core of the build.

At that point you've succeeded.

They're not just six collections of stats.

They're six different ways of playing Synthetic Ascension.

And I think that's the next evolution of your item system: rarity should answer "how strong is this item?"; behavioral item interactions should answer "what kind of lunatic has this run turned me into?"
