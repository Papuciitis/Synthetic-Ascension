## 1. Pale infill and watch surfaces — CLOSE

The right end panel in [guard-small](architecture/guard-small/exterior.png) and [guard-normal](architecture/guard-normal/exterior.png) now has restrained material variation, a darker plinth and visible edge treatment. The watch front has the same believable material language. Beside the original textured arches these faces no longer look like unfinished flat-colour placeholders. Keep the quiet treatment; adding more joints or outlines is unnecessary.

This closes the original surface finding, not every future material-quality opportunity.

## 2. Watch body, side and eave identity — CONTINUE

The recessed divided window, sill and deeper eave improve the read substantially: the front now looks like an occupied room rather than a vent grille. However, in both architecture sizes the tall slate cap still dominates the short exposed wall, and the right return reads as a narrow tapered strip blending into the main slate roof. At 960×540 it does not establish a sufficiently clear wall mass. The original vent/dormer finding is only partly resolved.

**Bounded correction:** keep the supported footprint/envelope, but make the existing return a continuous masonry/plaster side plane with a readable vertical front corner and a dark rear junction/contact shadow. Give that plane a value between the lit front and the dark roof rather than another slate-coloured sliver. If that alone cannot establish the room at small size, rebalance the visible cap/body division within the existing envelope. Do not substitute a brighter outline or extra ornament for visible wall mass. Recheck normal and small native views with optional props hidden.

An independent read-only architectural counter-review also closed action 1 and continued action 2; the verdict above is the coordinating designer's own inspection.

## 3. Actual side-entry cue — CONTINUE

The new treatment is much easier to locate than v1's thin roof-edge marks. It still reads as a separate pale rectangular slab or mat beside the roof edge in the [west exterior](native-entry-west/exterior.png), [east exterior](native-entry-east/exterior.png) and [recessed exterior](native-entry-irregular/exterior.png). Bright short bars cap its ends while a darker strip separates it from the visible covered opening. The cue attracts attention but does not yet read as one attached stone threshold belonging to the building. This is especially clear in the east and irregular restored views.

**Bounded correction:** join the same cue visibly to the real opening with a continuous sill/landing surface and short grounded stone returns at the actual jambs. Tone the broad pale surface and end bars toward the adjacent masonry so the opening's depth, jamb contacts and sill establish the entrance. Preserve the current opening, clear apron and existing physical data. No new step, blocking shape or alternate painted door is needed.

Use exterior/entered/restored images at the same actual west, east and recessed sites to confirm attachment. Add a clear native north-entry view when available; no north-entry image was supplied in this review, so north is not visually signed off. The entered views establish the relation between visible cue and actual opening; they do not by themselves make the exterior treatment convincing.
